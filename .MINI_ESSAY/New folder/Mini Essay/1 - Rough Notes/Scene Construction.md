# Scene Construction

**Study Guide: Understanding the Two Main Types of Scenes in Fiction**

---

### **Introduction**
- Most well-written scenes fall into one of two types.
- These scene types are universal across all fiction.
- Often, writers are not explicitly taught these structures.
- Purpose: To help writers understand how to structure scenes effectively and apply this knowledge to their work.

---

### **The Two Main Types of Scenes**

#### **1. Goal-Oriented Scene**
- **Definition:** A scene where a character has a specific goal.
- **Key Elements:**
  - Character starts with a clear motive.
  - Faces conflict and obstacles while pursuing the goal.
  - Ends in disaster or failure, preventing the character from achieving their goal.
- **Purpose:** Creates tension and drives the story forward.
- **Structure:**
  - Goal → Conflict → Disaster
- **Why ending in disaster?**  
  - Keeps the story engaging.
  - Sets up the subsequent reaction scene.

---

#### **2. Reaction Scene**
- **Definition:** A scene that follows a goal-oriented scene, focusing on the character's emotional response.
- **Key Elements:**
  - **Reaction:** The character's emotional response to the previous disaster.
  - **Dilemma:** The character considers options, which should be difficult or undesirable.
  - **Decision:** The character chooses a new goal or a different approach to the previous goal.
- **Purpose:** Develops character depth and emotional engagement.
- **Structure:**
  - Reaction → Dilemma → Decision

---

### **Structural Beats for Both Scene Types**
- **Six Key Points to Remember:**
  1. Goal (or emotional reaction)
  2. Conflict (or dilemma)
  3. Disaster (or decision)
- **Tip:** Write these six points down and keep them handy while writing.

---

### **Practical Application & Example**
- **Example:** *Raiders of the Lost Ark* (Indiana Jones)
  - **Goal Scene:** Indiana Jones tries to retrieve the golden idol.
    - Obstacles: betrayal, spiders, arrows, collapsing tomb, boulder.
    - Ends with him escaping but losing the idol to Belloc.
  - **Reaction Scene:** Indiana reflects on losing the idol.
    - Emotional response: frustration, determination.
    - Dilemma: whether to pursue the idol or talk to the army.
    - Decision: chooses to talk to the army, setting the story in motion.

---

### **Key Takeaways**
- Opening with a goal scene followed by a reaction scene creates a compelling narrative flow.
- Both scene types are essential for engaging storytelling.
- Incorporate the three structural points in each scene for clarity and impact.
