### Plot Frameworks

- **Promising, Progress, and Payoff:** Core structure for constructing plots.
- **Promising:** Establishing what the story is about and setting expectations.
- **Progress:** The movement of the story toward its goal.
- **Payoff:** The resolution that fulfills promises and surprises the reader.

---

### Promises in Storytelling

- **Definition:** The commitments made to the reader about what the story will deliver.
- **Types of promises:**
  - **Plot Promise:** What the story is about (e.g., destroy the ring).
  - **Tone Promise:** The mood or feel of the story (e.g., humorous, dark, whimsical).
  - **Character Promise:** What characters want or need, and their arcs.
  - **Series Promise:** What to expect across multiple books (e.g., a continuing adventure, a family saga).

- **How to make promises:**
  - Early in the story (first pages or chapters).
  - Through descriptions, character names, and initial scenes.
  - Using prologues or cold opens to set tone and expectations.

- **Signposting:** Clearly indicating progress and what’s coming next to keep the reader engaged.

---

### Handling Promises

- **Dropping or changing promises:** Acceptable if it serves the story; often involves "killing your darlings."
- **Revising promises:** Adjust promises after understanding the story’s ending or direction.
- **Between books/series:** Promises can be maintained through overarching themes or series promises (e.g., *Wheel of Time*).

---

### Progress in Storytelling

- **Control of progress:** Writers can manipulate time, events, and character development.
- **Signposting progress:** Use chapter beginnings, endings, and internal cues to show advancement.
- **Types of progress:**
  - **Information-based:** Discovering clues or solving mysteries.
  - **Relationship-based:** Characters growing closer or resolving conflicts.
  - **Action-based:** External obstacles overcome through physical action.
- **Pacing:** Match the story’s pace to its genre and style (thriller vs. slow burn).

---

### Pacing and Signposting

- **Consistency:** Varies based on style; thrillers often have rapid pacing, epics may slow down.
- **Variation:** Use breathers and faster sections to maintain interest.
- **Speeding up progress:** Use time jumps or quick summaries to skip travel or slow sections.

---

### Twists and Payoffs

- **Twist:** Surprising yet inevitable fulfillment of promises.
- **How to craft effective twists:**
  - Promise something, then escalate or over-deliver.
  - Use obstacles and escalation to build tension.
  - Signpost subtly or overtly to prepare the reader.
- **Payoff:** The resolution that feels both surprising and earned, fulfilling the promises made.

---

### Common Techniques

- **Cold open:** Start in media res to hook the reader.
- **Mini promises:** Small hints or problems introduced early that relate to the main plot.
- **Foreshadowing:** Plant clues for future payoffs.
- **Escalation:** Increase stakes gradually to keep tension.

---

### Examples from Literature and Film

- **The Lord of the Rings:** Promises about the journey, with a tone of epic adventure.
- **Guardians of the Galaxy:** The real story is about relationships, not just the quest.
- **Star Wars:** Promises about the destruction of the Death Star, with signposted progress.
- **Into the Woods:** Breaks traditional promises to subvert expectations, creating a more complex story.

---

### Handling Deviations and Rule-Breaking

- Breaking rules (e.g., skipping act 1, changing tone mid-story) can work if done intentionally and with purpose.
- Strong promises and signposting help maintain reader engagement even when conventions are bent.

---

### Final Tips

- **Worldbuilding:** Important but less critical than plot and character.
- **Character arcs:** Attach promises to character growth.
- **Series promises:** Maintain consistency across multiple books.
- **Flexibility:** Use promises and progress as analytical tools to fix story issues.
- **Story evolution:** Be willing to revise promises and plot points as the story develops.

---

### Summary

- Focus on **promise, progress, and payoff** to craft compelling stories.
- Use **signposting** to guide the reader through the story’s journey.
- Be deliberate with promises—what you promise influences reader satisfaction.
- Control **progress** to build tension and deliver satisfying payoffs.
- Don’t be afraid to **break rules** if it serves the story, but do so intentionally.
