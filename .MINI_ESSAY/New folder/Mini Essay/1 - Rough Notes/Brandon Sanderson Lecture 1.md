# Brandon Sanderson Lecture 1

You'll learn more about Writing by Writing your first book, youll know how to use your tools and which tools to ignore

Writing advice is as good as its ability to help you INDIVIDUALLY

Your goals is your own.

You should get the effect you want, and not the effect what other peolple said you should want

The Purpose of Art is to make the Artist's life more enriched

Be aggressive on your Writing Schedules

If you haven't finished at least 3 novels, yoiur job is not to sell books, but to write enough books, your job is to learn your process

The Product of your writing time is Yourself

You are the Artwork

The time you spend Writing will change you.

While you are writing the book, the Artist should be in control.

The moment you Edit, there should be a 2nd person.

The Business side of you shouldn't be in charge when you're writing 1st draft

But the Artistic side of you shouldn't be in charge when you're editing.

Revision is a hard skill to learn.

Writers who constantly Revise the early chapters tend to not make it.

Publishers don't like long books, except if ur already established

Ideas are Cheap

The things that you're bad at, that you have to force yourself to learn, or you will not be able to succeed professionally, 
tend to end up as your strengths. That won't help you because blah, blah, blah. My weaknesses 
become strengths. Yeah. I know. That's hard, and I don't want to do it. Right?

Your job is to figure out what makes you tick to continuously write shit

Revision suggestion: Is to make an Outling for ur revisions that has targeted goals

In programming there is a Buglist, treat Revision like Bugfixing. Starting with the most Game-breaking bugs, into major, and then into minor bugs.

Ex: This character doesn't work, and here is the plan on how to fix that character.

Writing Groups. Writing Groups are good for Feedbacks.

Writing Groups are for everyone, but Writing Groups can ruin your books.

Learn to take Feedback. It is a good tool.

Be careful when taking Feedbacks.

When presenting your work, do not defend yourself and not dillute your feedback.
