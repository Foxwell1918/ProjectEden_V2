# Rhyming Scheme

A Rhyme scheme is a pattern of thymes at the end of each line of a poem or song. It is usually referred to by using letters to indicate which lines rhyme. Lines with the same letter will rhyme with eachother.

example of ABAB rhyming scheme:

A. Bid me to weep, and I will weep.
B. While i have eyes to see.
A. And have none, yet i will keep.
B. A heart to weep for thee.

## Functions in Writing.

These rhyme patterns have various effects and can be used to:
1. **Control flow**
	 If every line has the same AAAA rhyme. The stanza will read as having a very quick flow. Whereas a rhyme scheme like ABCABC can be felt to unfold more slowly.
2. **Structure the poerm's messege and thought patterns.**
	 For example, a couplet with a thyme scheme of AABB lends itself to simpler direct ideas. Because the resolution comes in the very next line. Essentially these couplets can be thought of as self contained statements. This idea of thyme schemes reflecting a thought process is often discussed particularily regarding Sonnets
3. **Determine whether a stanza is balanced or not**

4. Help renforce the feeling being expressed.
	 If the writer wants to express stubbornness they may use tight structure thyme schemes. Whereas if one was writing about feeling lost. then a stanza would only have one rhyme XXAXXXA

The distinction is between rhyme schemes that apply to a single stanza and those that continue their pattern throughout an entire poem. There are also more elaborate related form.
