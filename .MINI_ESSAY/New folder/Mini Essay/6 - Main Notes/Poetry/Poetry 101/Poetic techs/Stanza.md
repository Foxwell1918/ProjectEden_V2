# Stanza

Stanza, is an italian word meaning "Room" or "Ruang" is a group of lines within a poem, usually set off from others by a blank line.

Stanzas can have regular rhyme and metrical schemes, but they're not requiring either.

ex:

The following short poem by Emily Dickinson has two stanzas of four lines each

*I had no time to hate, because*  A
*The grave would hinder me,*  B
*And life was not so ample I*  C
*Could finish enmity.*  B
  
*Nor had I time to love; but since*  A
*Some industry must be,*  B
*The little toil of love, I thought,*  C
*was large enough for me.* B

ex 2:

The following poem by Andrew John Young has three stanzas of six lines each:

*Frost called to the water Halt*  A
*And crusted the moist snow with sparkling salt;*  A
*Brooks, their one bridges, stop,*  B
*And icicles in long stalactites drop.*  B
*And tench in water-holes*  C
*Lurk under gluey glass-like fish in bowls.*  C
  
*In the hard-rutted lane*  A
*At every footstep breaks a brittle pane,*  A
*And tinkling trees ice-bound,*  B
*Changed into weeping willows, sweep the ground;*  B
*Dead boughs take root in ponds*  C
*And ferns on windows shoot their ghostly fronds.*  C
  
*But vainly the fierce frost*  A
*Interns poor fish, ranks trees in an armed host,*  A
*Hangs daggers from house-eaves*  B
*And on the windows ferny am bush weaves;*  B
*In the long war grown warmer*  C
*The sun will strike him dead and strip his armour.* C
