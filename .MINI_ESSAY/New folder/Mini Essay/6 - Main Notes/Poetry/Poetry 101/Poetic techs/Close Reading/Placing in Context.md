# Placing in Context

Don't think of Context as a bunch of historical and outside materials that you need to research for the poem, although you sometimes have to.

Think of these context as a Conversation. Many of the best poets live their lives in conversation in Poetry.

[[John Keats]] is one of the best example.

Keats's friend Charles Clark lent a copy of edmund spencer the fairy queen to him. Keatss first encounter wiht the book is like a wild horse running through the meadow.

He then return to it throughout his life.

What caught Keats the most are Spencer's [[Epithets TDL]].

his favourite is "A sea-shouldering whale" where he sat straight up and said "What an image that is!" it's a very thought provoking image.

A poem is a dialogue between you as the poet, with the poet that had preceeded you. Every artist steals, and a poet steals ideas from their predecessors and use it to communicate their ideas, and on and on poets will always communicate with their predecessors, as if i am talking to Aristotles right now.

Sometimes,

Context can come in the form of a [[Allusion]].
In English, Allusions are often Biblical or Classical
Meaning most Allusions are from either a Biblical or Classical text

Good example of classical Allusion is in Romeo and Juliet when Romeo describe his first love Rosalyn

"She'll not be hit with Cupid's arrow; she hath Dian's wit"

Allusions can enrich a poem with its associations

Allusions can pack a large amount of information in a small space.

Allusions are an author's way to communicate and draw upon a shared cultural knowledge. Which is a hugely accessible thing from your audience.
