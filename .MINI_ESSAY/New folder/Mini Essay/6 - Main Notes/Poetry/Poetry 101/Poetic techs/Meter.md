# Meter

A meter in poetry is the rhytmic structure of a line made up of two or more syllables and the pattern of emphasis or lack of emphasis on each of the syllables.

A poetic foor is a unit of a meter.

The most common metrical feet are Iambic, Trochaic, Spondaic, Anapestic, and Dactylic. Both rhythm and meter are important to poetry

---


A Metrie in poetry is the basic Rhythmic Structure of a Verse or Lines. 

Qualitative Metre are what we most familiar in English. With Stressed syllables coming at regular intervals, ex Iambic Pentameters

Some language used what known as Quantitative Meters. Where patterns were based on Syllable Weight rather than stress.

In many western classical poem, the metre of a verse can be described as a sequence of [[Metrical Foot]]. Each foot being a specific sequence of syllable types. such as Stressed vs Unstressed syllable, long or short.

An Iambic Pentameter is a common metre in Englsh Poetry based on five Iambs, each consisting of relativelt unstressed syllable
