# Metaphors 

The word Metaphor derives from 16th century old frienc word Metaphore, which comes from latin Metaphora, or "Carrying over" and also the Greek Metaphora that means "Transference of ownership"

in the book The Philosophy of Rhetoric, it is described that Metaphor has two parts:
1. The Tenor / Target
	 That is the subject to which atttributes are ascribed
2. The Vehicle / Source,
	 Is the object whose attributes are borrowed from.

### Metaphors vs Similes

A Metaphor assets the objects in the comparison are identitcal on the point of comparison. Whilst Simile merely asserts a simillarity through the words "Like or As" 

A metaphor is generally more forceful than simile.

Aristotle discusses the creation of metaphors at the end of his Poetic But the greatest thing by far is to be a master of metaphor. It is the one thing that cannot be learnt from others; and it is also a sign of genius, since a good metaphor implies an intuitive perception of the similarity in dissimilars."

### Psychological Effect

Psychological research shows that metaphors influence perception, reasoning and decision-making by shaping how people conceptualize abstract ideas.
