## Overview
Alchemy is a Magic System that alters the state of natural matter.

Alchemical spells change the  properties of a creature , object, an environment. They might turn an enemy into a harmless creature. bolster the strength of an ally, make an object move at the caster's command, ar enhance a creature's innate healing abilities lo rapidly recover from injury.

---

## Etymology
The word alchemy comes from  Old Argantheian_alkimie_, used in The Dark Age as _alchymia_. This name was itself adopted from the Alamirian word _al-kīmiyā_ (الكيمياء). The Arabic _al-kīmiyā_ in turn was a borrowing of the late Glyndorian term _khēmeía_ (χημεία), also spelled _khumeia_ (χυμεία) and _khēmía_ (χημία), with Al being the Alamirian definite article 'the',

Together this association can be interpreted as **'the process of Transmutation. by which to fuse or reunite with the divine or original form**'. Several etymologies have been proposed for the Glyndorian term. The first was proposed by Zosimos of Panopolis (3rd–4th centuries), who derived it from the name of a book, the _Khemeu_. Hermann Diels argued in 1914 that it rather derived from χύμα, used to describe metallic objects formed by casting.

## Sanderson’s First Law: "An author’s ability to solve conflict with magic is directly proportional to how well the reader understands said magic."
- **Rules & Mechanics:**  
  - You must know the movement of the molecules and can imagine it, in order to make the change in the object.  
  - By learning each molecules and movements of molecules. Alchemy is like math. think of how some people can quickly answer 12+11 is 23, but some people require to use their fingers or paper. How quick an Alchemist can cast a spell is like how fast a mathematician can come to a conclusion. 
  - Unlike other magical disciplines, what limits an Alchemist is one own's brain power, anyone who can harness magic can learn Alchemy. that is why Alchemy is also called "People's magic."
- **Costs of Use:**  
  - Materials, Brain Power.
- **Strengths of the System:**  
  - It is very empirical and doesn't require rituals, sacrifices, meditations, etc. Someone can know Alchemy whilst not really know much about the Arcane.

---

## Sanderson’s Second Law: "Weaknesses are more interesting than powers."
- **Limitations:**  
  - Alchemy can't transform what the Caster didn't know.
  - Boundaries of the system.  
- **Drawbacks / Side Effects:**  
  - What happens if the system is pushed too far (madness, mutations, burnout, ecological damage).  
- **Vulnerabilities:**  
  - Weaknesses in the practitioner (need training, reliance on tools, corruption, addiction).  
  - Weaknesses in the system itself (counter-magic, null zones, imbalance).
